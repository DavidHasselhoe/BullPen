# Earn the click, then earn the card

BullPen's best organic bet is data, not definitions. Ticker-level attribute pages (dividends, congress trades, institutional holders, "X vs Y" comparisons), server-rendered with its own data and linked from crawlable hub pages, are the query class AI Overviews touch least. Glossary-style explainers now lose roughly 58-61% of their clicks wherever an AI Overview appears. AI-search visibility tracks off-site brand mentions (Reddit, YouTube, "best app" roundups) about three times more closely than it tracks backlinks, so the same few channels feed human signups and AI citations at once. On conversion, every dataset agrees that a card-required trial converts trial-to-paid 2.5-5x better than a no-card trial (about 30-49% vs 8-18%). The data is almost all B2B, though, and the full-funnel effect (visitor to paid) is genuinely contested. Meanwhile BullPen's closest competitor, Simply Wall St, runs the alternative: a no-card, 7-day reverse trial that drops to a free plan. The evidence does not justify switching blind. It does justify a randomized test of card-required 7-day vs no-card reverse trial, judged on signup-to-paid at day 60 net of refunds. Several cheaper fixes do not need a test: deliver a Pro moment in the first session, stop blocking access on email confirmation, show a Blinkist-style trial timeline, and send the trial reminder early enough to satisfy Visa's reported 7-day notice. A realistic horizon for a two-week-old domain is 6-12 months before search matters. Conversion work pays back now.

## Data pages win search; glossary pages feed AI Overviews

The finance sites that win organic search split into two camps, and only one is a model for BullPen. Publishers like Investopedia earn traffic on educational definitions, and that is exactly the segment being eroded. Dotdash Meredith, Investopedia's owner, reported a **3% year-over-year drop in core sessions** in Q1 2025, partly blamed on AI Overviews, which appeared on roughly a third of searches tied to its content ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/dotdash-meredith-reports-google-ai-overviews-impact-traffic-in-q1-earnings/)). Data sites win on ticker pages and tools. Even there, the mature winner is mostly a brand. Stock Analysis drew about **6.09M visits in August 2026, 70.72% of them direct** and only about 1.26M organic, with its top organic keywords being its own name. It has roughly 15.16K referring domains behind it ([Semrush](https://semrush.com/website/stockanalysis.com/overview)). Its founder previously built Authority Nutrition, the base of Healthline's nutrition brand ([Stock Analysis about](https://stockanalysis.com/about/)), so this was a seasoned SEO operator compounding product quality into direct visits over seven years. No published case study explains its growth, so the "long tail of ticker sub-pages" reading is an inference from the traffic mix, not a disclosed fact.

The click economics have shifted hard against informational content. Ahrefs' December 2025 study of 300K keywords found that AI Overviews correlate with a **58% lower CTR for the #1 result**, up from 34.5% in its April 2025 study ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)). Seer Interactive measured a **61% organic CTR drop** across 3,119 informational queries ([PPC Land on Seer](https://ppc.land/google-ai-overviews-reduce-organic-ctr-61-paid-traffic-68/)). Pew's browsing panel saw users click a traditional result **8% of the time with an AI summary vs 15% without**, and only 1% clicked a link inside the summary ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)). The same Pew data supplies the strategic key. AI summaries appeared on **60% of question-format searches but only 8% of one-to-two-word searches**. "What is a P/E ratio" is a losing query. "NVDA dividend history", "AAPL institutional holders" and "MSFT vs GOOGL" are short, data-seeking queries that mostly still produce a classic results page. That makes BullPen's 116 glossary pages internal-link hubs and tooltip targets, not acquisition. A glossary entry that embeds live data ("P/E of the ten largest S&P 500 companies today") is more defensible than a definition.

The real risk on ticker pages is sameness, not page count. Google's scaled content abuse policy targets "many pages... generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users", explicitly including generative-AI pages "without adding value" ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). No volume threshold has been published ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/)). John Mueller said in September 2026 that programmatic SEO "often leads to a site that's either spam, borderline spam, or low quality". He added that once trust is lost, recovery "tends to take time and significant effort", and deleting the bad pages does not restore it automatically ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/search-seo/google-john-mueller-programmatic-seo-site-trust-recovery)). Around 519 ticker pages is not a scale red flag. Five hundred near-identical AI paragraphs, or a client-rendered shell with no data in the HTML, would be. BullPen's defence is data competitors' pages lack: the health score, signals, the Why Today explanation, Washington Trading congress trades, 13F holders and plain-language beginner framing. All of it has to be present in the server-rendered HTML for the policy to count it. Treat "is X a good buy" pages as both doorway-shaped and maximally YMYL, and keep weekly-pick pages either substantial or noindexed. Google judges sites, so weak templates drag strong ones down.

Finance is where core updates bite hardest. The December 2025 update hit finance sites from about December 14, with severe drops and surges across 3,000+ tracked sites. Glenn Gabe called publishing AI content without human editing "extremely risky" ([GSQi](https://gsqi.com/marketing-blog/google-december-2025-broad-core-update-analysis-findings)). Winners of the June 2025 update were independent sites "with clear author credentials, in-depth visuals, and well-organised layouts" ([Amsive](https://www.amsive.com/insights/seo/june-2025-core-update-winners-losers-trends/)). No study isolates the ranking value of bylines, but the cheap moves are obvious. Add a named human editor on Academy content, a methodology page explaining the health score and data sources (TwelveData, SEC filings), source citations on ticker pages, clear not-advice disclosures, and human review of AI text before it is indexed.

On timing, the ~630 "Discovered, currently not indexed" URLs on a two-week-old domain are normal. Google's crawl-budget guidance targets sites with 10K+ rapidly changing or 1M+ pages. For smaller sites, crawl demand follows "popularity" and perceived inventory ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/large-site-managing-crawl-budget)). Mueller names poor internal linking as a leading cause, and linking priority pages from higher in the architecture as the fix ([Positional](https://www.positional.com/blog/discovered-currently-not-indexed)). Site moves take months to settle even when done correctly. Practitioners monitor for at least 180 days ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-splitting-merging-website-can-cause-months-unstable-search-rankings/256789/)). The order of levers is inference, but it follows Google's own guidance. Start with homepage-linked hubs (S&P 500 list, sector pages, A-Z glossary) that each link their children. Then add peer, sector and compare cross-links between ticker pages, a first wave of external mentions, server-rendered data, and manual indexing requests for the top 20-50 URLs.

## Brand mentions beat backlinks three to one in AI answers

AI search is a small, high-quality channel for now. In June 2025, AI platforms sent **1.13B referrals to the top 1,000 sites, up 357% year over year**, against about **191B from Google Search**. ChatGPT accounted for over 80% of the AI share ([TechCrunch on Similarweb](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/25/ai-referrals-to-top-websites-were-up-357-year-over-year-in-june-reaching-1-13b/)). Conversion quality is the case for caring. AI search was 0.5% of Ahrefs' visitors but 12.1% of its signups, about **23x organic** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-traffic-conversions-ahrefs/)). Semrush models AI visitors at **4.4x** an organic visitor's value ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-search-seo-traffic-study/)). Ahrefs' audience is unusually AI-literate B2B, and Semrush's figure is partly a projection. No consumer-fintech dataset exists, so plan on "2-5x organic conversion, on tiny volume", never 23x.

The levers that move AI citations are mostly ordinary SEO plus off-site reputation. Google states there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode" and no special schema or AI text file is needed ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). OpenAI's docs say sites that block **OAI-SearchBot** will not appear in ChatGPT search answers. GPTBot is training-only, and robots.txt changes take about 24 hours ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). llms.txt is effectively inert. Mueller says "no AI system currently uses llms.txt", and one server-log study saw zero AI-crawler requests for it across 9,645 logged requests ([AWRSHIFT](https://wp.awrshift.com/llms-txt-in-practice/)). The strongest signal is reputation. Across 75K brands, AI Overview visibility correlated **0.664 with branded web mentions but only 0.218 with backlinks**, and 26% of brands had no AIO mentions at all ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). The most-cited domains across ChatGPT, AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity and AIO are **Reddit, then YouTube and LinkedIn** ([Search Engine Land on Peec AI](https://searchengineland.com/ai-search-engines-cite-reddit-youtube-and-linkedin-most-study-473138)). The citation long tail is fat: no single domain exceeds about 5% of citations ([Similarweb](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/most-cited-domains-llms/)). For BullPen, GEO means getting named in Reddit threads, YouTube reviews and existing "best stock research app / best AI investing app" listicles. It also means confirming robots.txt admits OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot, Googlebot and Bingbot, and tagging chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and gemini referrers in PostHog so the channel is measured, not guessed. The correlation data is not causal, and no study isolates freshness or schema as a citation factor.

## Referral, Reddit and YouTube pay twice; creators need guardrails

The one hard number in consumer investing growth comes from Robinhood's S-1. "Organic growth and the Robinhood Referral Program were responsible for **over 80% of the new Funded Accounts**" in 2020 and Q1 2021 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000162828021013318/robinhoods-1.htm)). That is organic and referral combined, not 80% from referral, a common misquote. The reward was a symmetric free share for both sides ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/ipo/2021/robinhood-filing-shows-continued-surge-in-retail-investing)). BullPen already has a referral program, so the lever is reward shape (symmetric free Pro time) and timing (prompting right after a win moment, such as a Why Today explanation or a pick that played out). Shareable artefacts that carry the brand and create crawlable pages are the natural viral objects, though no investing-specific share-loop data exists.

Reddit and YouTube are the two channels that pay twice, once in direct users and again as the most-cited AI sources. Reddit's sitewide norm is roughly 90/10 participation to promotion, and finance subreddits are stricter, often banning self-promotion outright ([redship](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules)). Those rules are second-hand and must be checked per sidebar, so the founder-name, answer-first approach is the only safe one. Gen Z investors learned investing from social media (48%) and search (47%) more than from professionals (30%), yet rate professionals twice as trustworthy ([CFA Institute/FINRA Foundation](https://www.cfainstitute.org/about/press-releases/2023/finra-cfa-institute-gen-z-research)). That argues for credible, educational presence over hype. Public.com reportedly reached 1M users in about 18 months on content plus community, but that figure comes from an agency case study, not a filing ([Mint Copywriting Studios](https://www.mintcopywritingstudios.com/blog/public-community-content-case-study)).

Product Hunt is a one-day event, not a channel. A top-3 finish brings about **5,000-15,000 visitors and 100-400 signups**, and a top-10 finish about 30-100 signups ([Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/product-hunt-traffic-data-2026)). One coordinated launch is worth it for the durable listing, which AI models can cite. Creator partnerships work in aggregate but deliver little per creator. M1 Finance got about **39,400 accounts from about 1,700 influencers** over three-plus years, roughly 23 each, and then paid FINRA's first finfluencer fine, **$850,000**, over unbalanced, promissory posts ([InvestmentNews](https://investmentnews.com/regulation-and-legislation/finra-fines-m1-finance-in-landmark-social-media-enforcement/250948)). As a non-broker, BullPen sits mainly under FTC endorsement rules, which require a clear disclosure of any material connection that the viewer sees before engaging ([Adclear](https://www.adclear.ai/blog/finfluencer-marketing-sec-finra-compliance)). The right structure is revenue-share with many micro-creators, mandatory disclosure, and a ban on performance claims. The Weekly Pick track record is the asset creators should not amplify, since it reads as promissory advice. Instagram auto-carousels and Discord cost little to keep running, but no evidence shows they acquire users, so measure them with UTMs before expanding.

## The card question is open; the reverse trial deserves a test

The trial-to-paid gap is large and consistent. First Page Sage's 86-company dataset shows **48.8% for card-required vs 18.2% for no-card** ([Shno.co](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/free-trial-conversion-statistics)). ChartMogul and Kyle Poyar's January 2026 report on 200 products found card-required trials convert **30%, "more than 5x"** the no-card rate. Only 20% of trial products require a card, and 7% now run reverse trials ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/free-to-paid-conversion-report)). The question that matters is visitor-to-paid, and there the sources diverge. Multiplying First Page Sage's own figures gives no-card at about **1.55% visitor-to-paid** (8.5% start × 18.2%) vs card-required at **1.22%** (2.5% × 48.8%). ChartMogul's per-1,000-visitor model gives card-required about **1.05% vs 0.36%** ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)). The datasets disagree on how many trial starts the card form kills: about 3.4x in one and 1.3x in the other. Both are B2B at 4-20x BullPen's price. At $12 a month the card form is a larger share of the decision, which likely makes the start penalty bigger for BullPen than either dataset shows, though no consumer card/no-card split exists.

Card-required trials also convert some intent that turns into regret. About 80% of US consumers in a September 2026 survey said they had been charged for a forgotten trial. Recurly's chief customer officer warned that surprise lock-in risks "ruining your chances of having them be a customer ever again" ([Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/forgotten-trials-paid-subscriptions-loyalty-suffers/830942/)). RevenueCat sees refunds of **5.8% on hard paywalls vs 3.4% on freemium** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)). For a beginner-investor brand built on trust, that cost is not captured in trial-to-paid.

High-priced incumbents ($199-299/yr) use card-required 7-day trials. The beginner-friendly, visual, freemium-first player closest to BullPen uses a no-card reverse trial. The reverse-trial evidence is mostly practitioner claims. Elena Verna reports a **10-40% lift in freemium-to-premium conversion** (about 20% typical when paid features are actually used in the window) ([Elena Verna](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/10-pricing-and-packaging-optimizations)), and notes pure freemium tops out near 5% ([Elena Verna](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/reverse-trials-examples)). Amplitude's 15% reverse-trial target is a goal, not a result ([Amplitude](https://amplitude.com:443/blog/reverse-trial)). Two conditions decide whether it works for BullPen. The free plan must be good enough to keep people after the downgrade, and BullPen's is. The loss must be felt, which only happens if the trial pushes users into Pro features (a Deep Dive on a held stock, a sixth alert, the daily brief) while it lasts. The cost side is real: every signup gets Pro AI usage. Cap AI consumption per trial user or measure cost per reverse-trial user first.

Trial length has better consumer data than the card question. RevenueCat's August 2025-July 2026 app-store analysis puts monthly-plan trial-to-paid at **39.6% for ≤4 days, 45.9% for 5-9, 46.6% for 10-16 and 43.7% for 17-32 days**. Annual-plan conversion keeps climbing, from **24% to 44.6%**, and AI apps peak at 5-16 days then fall to 31.8% beyond ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length)). Seven days is near the plateau for monthly. Fourteen would likely lift the annual plan BullPen pushes, and would cover two Weekly Picks and about ten trading days of briefs. Going past about 16 days adds model cost without lift. Those are app-store trials with a stored payment method, so they overstate what a Stripe card form gets.

Compliance tilts the calculus further. The FTC's Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated on July 8, 2025, but ROSCA remains enforceable and the FTC restarted rulemaking on March 11, 2026 ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/07/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-negative-option-rule); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/3/ftc-seeks-comment-on-potential-updates-to-negative-option-rule)). California's amended Automatic Renewal Law brings "free-to-pay conversions" explicitly in scope from July 1, 2025 ([Kilpatrick Townsend](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2024/10/california-latest-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-in-july-2025)). It also requires annual-renewal reminders and online click-to-cancel ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-06-04-california-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-on-july-1-2025)). Mastercard requires a reminder **3-7 days** before trial end ([Chargeback Gurus](https://www.chargebackgurus.com/blog/mastercard-subscription-rules)). Visa's rule is reported as **at least 7 days** before expiry ([TrustPay summary](https://www.trustpay.eu/?p=6933)), which BullPen's 3-day reminder on a 7-day trial cannot meet literally. The primary Visa document did not render, so verify whether the rule applies only to longer trials. Until then, send a reminder at trial start plus one on day 4 or 5. From June 19, 2026, EU law requires a "withdraw from the contract here" button throughout the 14-day withdrawal period, including for non-EU sellers ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/05/eu-withdrawal-button-uk-subscription-rules-and-data-protection-risks-for-us-online-sellers)). As an EEA company, BullPen should assume it applies. A no-card reverse trial sidesteps nearly all of this because no negative-option conversion occurs. For a one-person team that is a real operational argument, separate from conversion.

## Activation, not the paywall, decides the first session

Almost everything happens on day 0. RevenueCat finds **82% of trial starts on install day** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)). Adapty puts it at **89.4%**, and says a user who misses the first session has only an 11% chance of ever starting ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-personalize-onboarding-and-paywalls-in-your-mobile-app/)). Cancellations front-load too: **39.8% of 7-day-trial cancellations happen on day 0** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Amplitude finds that getting just **7% of a cohort back on day 7** is top-quartile, and 69% of top day-7 performers remain top performers at three months ([Amplitude](https://www.amplitude.com/blog/7-percent-retention-rule)). Before the end-of-onboarding offer, the user should already have seen a real Pro output on their own stock: a Why Today explanation, or a brief built from their holdings. On trial start, generate one immediately so the day-0 canceller meets value first. The aha event should be chosen from data, not instinct. Candidates are the first Why Today read on a held or watched ticker, the first Ask Bull answer about one, and holdings added. Pick whichever best separates day-30 retained from churned users in PostHog, then confirm causally by pushing more users to it. Personalised onboarding has repeated modest wins in mobile case studies, for example **+8.5% trial starts and +17% paid conversions** ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-personalize-onboarding-and-paywalls-in-your-mobile-app/)), but only when quiz answers visibly change what the user sees next.

BullPen stacks gates before value: email confirmation, the age gate, a multi-step quiz, then a card form. The age gate is a legal decision and stays. Email-confirmation-before-access has the weakest justification of the rest, and the pattern against it is consistent, though no recent numeric A/B exists. Duolingo moved signup behind a first lesson and gained about **20% DAU** ([Taplytics](https://taplytics.com/blog/duolingo-ab-test-onboarding)). Baymard finds **26% of shoppers abandon** when forced to create an account ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux)). Reddit saw **+50% signups from Sign in with Google and another +90% from One Tap** ([Google Identity](https://developers.google.com/identity/sign-in/case-studies/reddit?authuser=5)). The tests are: let people in before confirming (gate only sending features until confirmed), add One Tap on public stock pages, and keep the signed-out gates firing at moments of intent (save, ask Bull) rather than up front.

Paywall placement is already right; paywall content is the opportunity. The onboarding paywall with a trial is the best-converting placement in Adapty's data ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-personalize-onboarding-and-paywalls-in-your-mobile-app/)), and contextual limit gates are the second leg. Hard paywalls convert about **5x freemium** on download-to-paid (10.7% vs 2.1%) with near-identical year-1 retention ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). That is the price of BullPen's reach-first model, not a reason to abandon it. Showing the product beats describing it. Unchaind's switch from a text paywall to a visual preview raised conversion **over 50%** ([Superwall](https://superwall.dev/case-studies/unchaind)), while blurred previews are rare and untested ([Lazyweb](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/are-blurred-preview-paywalls-actually-common)). A Why Today or Deep Dive limit dialog should show a real, complete first section for that stock and end at a clear boundary ("Pro shows the full breakdown"), never a mid-sentence ellipsis, per BullPen's own no-truncation rule. For the card-required trial, the most transferable tested result is Blinkist's trial timeline ("Today: full access. Day 5: we remind you. Day 7: charged, cancel anytime"). It lifted **trial starts 23% and cut cancellations 4%** by answering the fear of forgetting ([UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/how-solving-our-biggest-customer-complaint-at-blinkist-led-to-a-23-increase-in-conversion-b60ad514134b)), and it doubles as the compliance reminder.

## Annual plans retain 2.6x better, so frame the $108 honestly and push it

The plan-interval evidence is stronger than the copy evidence. Annual subscriptions show **44.1% median year-1 retention vs 17.0% for monthly** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)). AI apps earn 41% more per payer but churn **30% faster** ([PPC Land on RevenueCat](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)), which makes annual more important for BullPen than for a typical tool. Annual adoption is falling, from 41.4% to 33.6% in a year ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)), so the default and framing matter. In a vendor case, adding a discounted annual plan beside monthly raised proceeds per user 80% ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/solutions/increase-revenue-arpu-ltv)). The popular "2 months free beats save 20%" claim has no primary experiment behind it, and Mida finds price-framing tests beat control only **11% of the time** ([Mida](https://mida.so/tests/use-case-grok)). BullPen's $9 vs $12 is a 25% saving, exactly three months free. Default the toggle to annual and test "3 months free" against "$9/mo billed yearly". Keep the $108 charged today as prominent as the $9 equivalent, which California's ARL scrutiny effectively requires.

Trust copy is cheap and category-standard. Simply Wall St's pricing page leads with "Cancel directly from your account settings in two clicks", "The price you see is the price you pay", a six-question FAQ and real app-store ratings ([Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/plans)). Koyfin, TipRanks and Motley Fool all offer 30-day money-back guarantees ([Koyfin](https://www.koyfin.com/pricing/); [TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/plans)). BullPen already has a 30-day refund window, so it should say so next to the button. Social proof must be real. The FTC's fake-review rule (effective October 21, 2024) bans AI-generated and undisclosed insider testimonials, with penalties around $52,000 per violation ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). For a young product, that means real counts and real permissioned quotes, or nothing. Upgrade copy should sell understanding and time saved ("know why your stock moved"), not outcomes. The Investment Advisers Act publisher exclusion protects impersonal commentary ([Lowe v. SEC](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181)), and features that read a user's holdings move closer to personalised advice, so get counsel to review paywall copy.

On price level, $12/mo sits just above the mobile North America median of $9.99 ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)) and at the bottom of standalone research tools. Simply Wall St Premium is about $10.95/mo, Morningstar $249/yr and Seeking Alpha $299/yr ([Wall Street Survivor](https://www.wallstreetsurvivor.com/?p=31812); [Freenance](https://freenance.io/products/morningstar-investor-review-2026-premium-mutual-fund-etf-research/); [Financer](https://financer.com/invest/seeking-alpha-premium-review/)). Higher-priced apps convert download-to-trial at 9.8% vs 4.3% for cheap ones ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)), but that is correlational. Price is not BullPen's conversion bottleneck. An upward test (for example $15, or $12 billed annually) belongs after activation is fixed, not before.

A card-required trial-to-paid rate below about 30% means the trial is not delivering a Pro moment early. Overall signup-to-paid should be judged against the 2-8% freemium range, not trial numbers. The events needed are signup started, completed and email confirmed, each onboarding step, trial offer viewed and started, the chosen aha event, paywall viewed (tagged with placement and feature), checkout started, subscription started (plan, interval), and trial cancelled with its day number.

## Conclusion

The two halves of this question turn out to be one problem. A beginner-investor app earns search traffic, AI citations and paid conversions through the same asset: visible, specific, trustworthy output about a stock the person cares about. A ticker page full of unique data ranks and gets cited. A Why Today explanation shown before the paywall converts. A Reddit answer that links one does both. The informational content founders reach for first (glossaries, "is X a good buy" posts, mass calculators) is exactly what AI Overviews absorb and Google's scaled-content policy punishes. The trust-eroding conversion tactics (surprise charges, fabricated social proof, outcome promises) carry fresh legal exposure under ROSCA, California's ARL, card-network rules and the FTC's review rule.

The largest unknown is the most consequential decision: card-required trial or no-card reverse trial. The published evidence is B2B, conflicting on the metric that matters, and silent on consumer finance. The closest competitor's choice is a signal, not proof. That makes BullPen's own A/B test more valuable than any benchmark in this report. At low volume it will only detect a large difference, and paid conversion in the low single digits needs hundreds of signups per arm, so start it early and let it run. Run it before search traffic arrives, so the funnel that eventually receives that traffic is already the right one.
